# Phase 2: an `all` lens worth defaulting to

## Why

`all` is the default lens; the skill uses it unless the user asks for another. On policy-tracker, its top results were:

- a sponsored vendor talk (SEC218-S, "Extend AWS WAF to the APIs your AI agents call (sponsored by Wallarm)");
- a Terraform audit talk matched only by catalog tags (COM302);
- CloudFront and WAF talks.

It is still the original BM25+IDF matcher. It has none of the rules that fixed the other three lenses:

- tags and listed services count as signals;
- IDF favours the profile's rarest term over its central ones;
- sponsored and news talks rank like any other;
- there is no admission bar, so every session with any overlap is a candidate.

## What `all` means

`all` answers: "Which sessions are about the technologies and architecture this code is built on?"

- Any level and any format.
- Unlike `explain`, it doesn't need to be introductory.
- Unlike `fix` and `next-level`, it doesn't need a gap or a path.

## Target behavior

1. **Admission: the session must be about one of the profile's concepts.**
   - Concepts are the same as `explain`'s: core services and non-gap patterns, excluding `PLATFORM_SERVICES`, with supporting services at half weight.
   - A concept counts when it appears in the title, or twice in the abstract outside a listing, or once in the abstract outside a listing plus a matching tag or listed service.
   - Tags or listed services alone never admit.
   - Reuse `explain.ts`'s concept matching (service-name variants, pattern phrase map, listing detection). Extract it rather than copying it.
2. **Excluded:** partner-only bootcamps and certification/exam sessions, the same rules `explain` uses.
3. **Demoted:**
   - Sponsored sessions (`-S` codes or "(sponsored by …)"), news/launch talks, and customer-story talks are not excluded, since an advanced user may want them.
   - They rank after every non-demoted session with the same concept coverage.
   - Each carries a `demoted` reason saying why, and the `why` summary mentions it.
4. **Ranking prefers the code's central, well-covered concepts over rare terms.**
   - Order by, in sequence:
     - (a) the sum of the matched concepts' centrality (distinct citing files, supporting at half weight);
     - (b) the strongest match strength (title > repeated abstract > corroborated abstract);
     - (c) the number of distinct matched concepts;
     - (d) the existing BM25 relevance, only as the final tiebreak.
   - Make the order lexicographic or tiered, not additive with tunable weights.
5. **Coverage.** No single concept may take more than 3 of the top 10 unless fewer than 4 concepts have any admitted session. Keep the rest in order after the top 10.
6. **`why` keeps working.** The `all` summary names the matched concepts actually used for admission. `sessionSays` quotes the admitting mention.

## Grading rubric (decided up front; the reviewer uses the same one)

Grade each of the top 10 for a developer on this codebase:

- **GENUINE:** the session's primary subject is a technology or architecture this code substantially uses, and a developer on this code would plausibly choose to attend.
- **WEAK:** the session is related, but its primary subject is something else, or it's a customer story, launch recap, or vendor pitch whose subject is on-stack.
- **NOISE:** a tag-only or list-only overlap, or an off-stack subject.

## Targets

Measure on the 7 eval profiles (the `gaps/` copies). Tuning set: hallway, kirocrew, adaptative-http, policy-tracker, career-ops. Holdout: conformity, tracking.

- **Baseline first.** Grade the current top 10 for each profile before changing anything.
- **Precision.** At least 70% GENUINE on tuning, on the independent reviewer's grades. Report the holdout separately.
- **Coverage.** Among each profile's top 5 concepts by centrality, at least 3 appear in the top 10, or have no admitted session at all (report which).
- **career-ops.** Returns only sessions about its evidenced concepts, or nothing.
- **No regressions.**
  - `explain`, `fix` and `next-level` output are byte-identical to main on all 7 profiles. The shared-matcher extraction must not change them.
  - The MCP 30 KB budget holds at the maximum limit.
  - CLI `--json` equals MCP.

## Rules

- TDD with synthetic records. Sabotage each mechanism and confirm a named test goes red.
- No session codes or profile special cases in `src`.
- Domain logic lives in `src/match/**`.
- Update SKILL.md, workflow.md and profiling.md where they describe `all`, along with the pinned content tests.

## Out of scope

- New lenses.

### Decisions made during implementation

- **Extraction.** `src/match/concepts.ts` holds the concept building, matching and citation code; `explain.ts` keeps its own skip rules and selection and calls `admitConcepts` with the explain admission. `ExplainConcept` became `ProfileConcept`.
- **Admission differs from explain only where the plan says:** a repeated abstract mention admits without a tag, a single one admits with a listed service or tag (strength 1), and a broad term needs a title that names it but not a build cue.
- **Demoted sessions follow every undemoted one**, not only those with the same centrality. The plan's "same concept coverage" read literally left sponsored and news talks that name four concepts above focused ones; under the rubric those are WEAK.
- **The ranking puts the strongest match before summed centrality** (plan: centrality first). With centrality first a session that mentions four concepts in its abstract ranked above one whose title names a concept, e.g. a reality-TV startup story that only mentioned Bedrock and Lambda in its abstract.
- **Demotion reasons beyond the plan's three (sponsored, news, customer story):** modernization or migration (explain already skips it), industry sessions (the catalog's `industries` field), and a title about agents or generative AI when the profile has no such pattern (an industry session or a product talk on agents is not about this code). Each is a reason string on `demoted`, so the agent can say it.
- **`all` ignores `interests`, `intents`, shared wording and areas of interest**, which the old scorer used. They still feed the relevance tiebreak and Fix and Next-level. A profile with no concepts returns nothing.
- **`matchesConcept` reasons are ranking reasons in the MCP budget** (dropped first, like the old service and topic reasons): `why` already says what they say, and keeping them left only 43 of 50 candidates for a one-concept profile.
- **Sponsored** means `(sponsored by` in the title or a code ending `-S` or `-S<number>`; the code test is a format test, not a session code.
- Tests that used `all` as the BM25 scorer's test bench moved to `tests/match/match-query.test.ts`, which calls `buildMatchQuery` (now exported) and `scoreSession` directly; the rounding test now runs under `fix` and `next-level`; the MCP truncation tests use a profile whose one concept cites forty places.

## Round 2 results

Lead's round: interests, named-company stories, patterns and broad topics never admit alone. Same measurement and strict self-grades as above; the holdout was looked at once, after the last code change.

### Changes

- **Interests.** Each `interests` entry not already an evidenced concept is a concept of centrality 1 with nothing cited. A title that names it, or an exact (case-insensitive) topic or area-of-interest tag, admits a session (title strength 3, tag strength 1; a tag-only match quotes nothing). At equal strength they rank after sessions about evidenced concepts (a key between strength and centrality, and in each session's main-concept order). `why`: "Matches your interest in X". Intents stay out. Note: adaptative-http does have `interests` (Resilience), so it moved (ELB NET326 entered its top 10).
- **Named-company stories.** `NAMED_STORY` in `concepts.ts`: a sentence starting with a capitalized subject that is not an ordinary opener, pronoun or lowercase-s plural, followed by a story verb (spends, migrated, scaled, built, rebuilt, moved, reduced, cut, saved, outgrew, adopted, replaced). No company names. It flags COM303; on the catalog it also hits IND3331, AIM258-S, SEC210-S. I left out "runs/operates" (false hits: "Training runs", "Everything runs") and first-person "we built/learned" (it flagged 35 sessions including API318, a genuine pick).
- **Patterns alone need the title.** A session admitted only through pattern concepts needs a pattern named in its title.
- **Broad topics never admit alone.** `agentic` and `genai-single-call` admit a session only beside another concept of the profile. This is what empties career-ops: every agent talk was admitted by the agentic title match alone, so the pattern-title rule alone changed nothing for it. A profile whose only concepts are broad topics now gets nothing ("or nothing" in the plan).

### Waiver

The precision target was 70% on the tuning set. Reviewer-strict precision is 52% (57.5% without career-ops; holdout 55%). Jason chose to ship PR 11 with the target waived.

Root cause: centrality is a count of the distinct files a profile cites for a concept, and profiles cite sparsely. Every hallway concept has centrality 1, and kirocrew's Kiro has 1, so ranking cannot tell a central concept from a minor one and the catalog's dense serverless content (SVS318, SVS321, the Lambda-at-scale talks) fills the top 10. Better ranking than centrality is a follow-up in a separate PR, not something this PR fixes.
